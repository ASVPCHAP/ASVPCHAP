## 2. Myron Golden's principles

### 2.1 Believe the next video could go big
- Your next video could get 40 million views, regardless of how long you've been doing this, how much you know or how many subscribers you have.
- But only if you **build it in a way that it could**. A video that gets 40 million views has to be one that about 400 million people would stop for, click on, and watch for the first 30 seconds.
- Believing comes before understanding. Package every video as if it might take off.

### 2.2 Think *from* the market, not *of* the market
- Stop thinking about the people you want to attract and the products you want to sell. Start thinking **from those people's point of view: what outcome do they want?**
- The common mistake: people pick a topic they want to talk about, make the video they want to make, design a thumbnail that looks cool to them, and wonder why nobody watches. **"You are not your audience."**
- Ask "What could I create that tens of millions of people would lean into?" instead of "What should my niche be?"

### 2.3 Start with a big idea
- A **big idea** is one a very large part of the market would care about enough to look at.
- It needs at least one of the **five C's**:
  1. **Curiosity**
  2. **Contrast**
  3. **Controversy**
  4. **Conflict**
  5. **Confusion** (the good kind)
- One is the minimum, two is better, three or more is better still. With all five it's "virtually impossible" for someone not to at least stop.

### 2.4 Thumbnails need a "wait, what?" factor
- The thumbnail is what makes people stop scrolling, but only if it makes them think "wait, what?"
- **Don't put a question on the thumbnail.** Most people think a question creates curiosity. It doesn't. It creates bad confusion: if viewers don't know the answer, they feel stupid and scroll past. "Nobody wants to watch a video that makes them feel stupid."
- **Make it readable on a phone.** Most people watch YouTube on their phones, where thumbnails are tiny. Use an easy-to-read font that's not too thin and doesn't blend into the background. Older viewers often can't read small thumbnail text without glasses.
- **Examples he praised:**
  - A golf thumbnail reading "CHEAT CODE 30/50/70": it promised a specific shortcut for short-game distances. 57K views in 3 days, even though nobody knew who the golfer was.
  - A self-defense channel's "He stopped the school shooter illegally": it combined curiosity, contrast, conflict and controversy. About 3M views in 2 days.
  - His own "Beat the Best Investors" (photos of Buffett, Munger, Musk, Gates and Bezos on one side, himself on the other).

### 2.5 The title delivers what the thumbnail teases
- The thumbnail creates the curiosity. **The title promises to resolve it.** Example: thumbnail "Beat the Best Investors" plus title "The Ultimate Investment Strategy."
- If both the thumbnail and the promise are something a lot of people care about, the video is more likely to do well.

### 2.6 Broad on the outside, narrow on the inside
- **Make the thumbnail and title for "John Q. Public"** (everyone), not only for your ideal customer.
- Then narrow from the big idea down to your niche inside the video. **The right people pick themselves, and everyone else leaves.** That's the intended result.

### 2.7 Know which kind of content you make
Myron's three types:
1. **Entertainment:** the most views, because most people use YouTube to be distracted (e.g. MrBeast).
2. **Education:** teaches something, so it only interests people who want to learn that thing.
3. **Enterprise:** business and entrepreneurship. The fewest views (maybe 7% of people own a business) but **much higher CPM/RPM**. He cited a creator with a **$176 CPM**.

**Smaller audiences can be more valuable audiences.** Don't measure a business channel against entertainment channels.

### 2.8 Count leads, not clicks
- The way to grow any business: **lead generation → lead conversion → customer ascension (selling them more) → customer retention.**
- To a creator who reported clicks: **"I don't want to know about clicks. I want to know about leads."** Track email signups.
- His own example: about 1,494 leads in 30 days, and about $56K in book sales in four weeks.

### 2.9 Go live with your ideal viewer
- Before he had in-person audiences, he taught people live on **Zoom and streamed it to YouTube**. Talking to real faces is easier than talking to a camera, and it attracts the ideal viewer.

### 2.10 Turn your service into a product
- There are only two kinds of businesses: **product businesses and service businesses.** Services are hard to grow because every sale takes your time.
- **Productize part of the business:** a course, a fixed-format program, physical products. His $55K/year mastermind is technically a service, but it's packaged like a product: fixed meetings, and it takes him the same time no matter how many people join.
- Applied to the barber in the Q&A: instead of "watch me cut hair," sell a $697 course on how to be a *wealthy* barber.

### 2.11 Other points from the session
- **Go deep:** be "an inch wide and a mile and a half deep" in your core knowledge. If you're afraid of running out of things to say, you don't know enough yet.
- **Write a great book:** a strong first step for anyone who knows their subject. Not "AI slop."
- **AI is leverage, a tool for your gaps:** use it for the part of the business that's currently hard (for example, making thumbnails if you don't know design). Leverage means a small input that produces a multiplied output.
- **A speaking business is not the same as being a good speaker:** know your business model. As fee, free, or hybrid speakers, start with the path that gets you paid fastest.
- **"Now you know" list:** when a collaborator turns out to be a taker, quietly stop working with them. No drama.

## 4. Checklist before publishing any video

**Idea**
- [ ] Would a very large audience stop for this, not just Anthony's niche?
- [ ] Is it written from the viewer's point of view (the outcome they want), not from what Anthony wants to talk about?

**Thumbnail**
- [ ] Uses at least two of: curiosity, contrast, controversy, conflict, confusion (good kind)
- [ ] **Not a question**
- [ ] 4 words or fewer, thick font, high contrast, readable at phone size

**Title**
- [ ] Promises the payoff that the thumbnail teases
- [ ] Short; no trailing phrase that dilutes the conflict

**Lead capture**
- [ ] Free download linked in the first line of the description and in a pinned comment
- [ ] Mentioned at about 2:00 and at the end

**After publishing**
- [ ] Log views, subscribers and the link in the tracker
- [ ] Every Sunday: log total subscribers and new email signups for the week, and note which topic area is winning
